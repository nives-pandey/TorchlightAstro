Western Astrology
This system is one of the oldest forms of astrology, with its earliest origins tracing back to Mesopotamia in the 2nd or 3rd millennium BCE. The Babylonians are credited with developing the initial zodiac and associating celestial events with earthly matters around 1700 BCE.

Western astrology was later refined by Greek and Roman thinkers. The Greek philosopher Ptolemy's work, the Tetrabiblos, written in the 2nd century CE, is a foundational text that codified many of the principles of horoscopic astrology still in use today.

Vedic Astrology (Jyotish)
Vedic astrology, or Jyotish, originated in ancient India. Its roots are in the Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism, which are believed to have been composed between 1500 and 1200 BCE. The Rig Veda, one of the four Vedas, contains details about the influence of celestial bodies on human life. One of the earliest dedicated texts on the subject is the Vedanga Jyotisha, which may date back to 1200 BCE. The system was further developed over centuries by sages such as Parasara, Jaimini, and Varahamihira.

Chinese Astrology
The origins of Chinese astrology can be traced back to the Zhou dynasty (1046–256 BCE), and it flourished during the Han dynasty (206 BCE to 220 CE). While myths suggest that the lunar calendar was invented by Emperor Huangdi in 2637 BCE, the 12 animal signs of the zodiac were first officially identified during the Han Dynasty. The earliest complete written reference to the 12 zodiac signs in China comes from a 6th-century translation of a Buddhist text, The Sutra of the Perfection of Wisdom.

Numerology
The belief that numbers hold mystical significance is an ancient concept with roots in several early civilizations, including Babylon and Egypt, dating back thousands of years. The earliest known references to numerology come from Babylon around 2000 BCE.

Modern numerology, particularly the Western system, is most often credited to the Greek mathematician and philosopher Pythagoras, who lived around 570–495 BCE. He and his followers believed that numbers were the fundamental building blocks of the universe. Another ancient system, Chaldean numerology, originated in Mesopotamia and is considered one of the oldest forms.

Human Design
Human Design is a modern system with very recent origins. It was created by Alan Robert Krakower, who went by the pseudonym Ra Uru Hu. He claimed to have had a mystical experience in 1987 where the system was revealed to him over eight days and nights. He published The Human Design System in 1992. It is not an ancient practice but a synthesis of several older traditions, including Western and Vedic astrology, the Chinese I Ching, the Hindu-Brahman chakra system, and the Kabbalah Tree of Life, combined with principles of modern science like physics
